Artifact 01 · AI & ML Timeline

The Evolution of Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning

AI & ML research · 2026

Research & visual communication

Introduction

Artificial intelligence has developed through many waves of progress and setbacks. I made this timeline to see the bigger picture: how early ideas about machine intelligence eventually led to the AI tools we use today.

Description

The visual groups major milestones into four periods: foundations of AI (1950–1969), AI winters and expert systems (1970–1993), machine learning and deep learning (1994–2016), and generative and modern AI (2017–2026).

Objective

To understand the turning points in AI and machine learning, including the periods when progress slowed, and explain them in a format people can scan and follow.

Process

I started by looking at major milestones in AI history, then grouped them into four broad periods. I chose examples that helped explain what changed in each period and shortened the descriptions so they would fit a visual timeline. The hardest part was deciding what to leave out without losing the overall story.

Tools and technologies used

Research and source review; ChatGPT for brainstorming and wording assistance; digital infographic design for the final visual. The exact infographic-design platform is not specified here.

Value proposition

For a technical audience, this timeline shows how I sort through a large topic and make the important points easy to follow. That same skill is useful when I explain data pipelines, new tools, or engineering choices to people with different backgrounds.

Unique value

What makes the timeline useful to me is that it includes both breakthroughs and setbacks. The AI winters, for example, make it clear that progress does not always move in a straight line.

Relevance to my audience

For engineering teams and hiring managers, this project offers a small but concrete example of how I learn a new technical subject and communicate it. It also connects my data engineering work with the AI/ML concepts I am studying.

Artifact 02 · Conversational AI Chatbot

Data Engineering Buddy: A Chatbot for Beginners

Conversational AI · Prompt design and response evaluation · 2026

Applied AI & technical communication

Try the chatbot. Ask it to explain ETL or a data pipeline using an online shopping example, then compare how it explains the concept to someone with no SQL experience.

Open Data Engineering Buddy ↗

Introduction

I created Data Engineering Buddy for a class activity focused on building and testing an AI chatbot. I chose a topic I work with regularly, but designed the conversation for someone who is just starting to learn data engineering.

Description

The chatbot answers beginner questions about ETL and data pipelines. I tested explanations using familiar online shopping scenarios and compared responses for people with different levels of technical knowledge.

Objective

To see whether a conversational AI tool could make foundational data engineering concepts easier to understand without relying on unnecessary technical terms.

Process

I prepared prompts about ETL, data pipelines, and explaining a pipeline without assuming SQL knowledge. I then reviewed the responses for clarity, relevance, and whether the examples would make sense to a beginner. Comparing answers helped me identify where a response needed more context or simpler wording.

Tools and technologies used

Botpress for the shareable chatbot experience; conversational AI; prompt design; response testing and evaluation. This is a learning prototype, not a production data pipeline or a custom-trained language model.

Value proposition

The project connects my data engineering experience with a practical use of AI: helping someone understand a technical process before they have to build or maintain it.

Unique value

Rather than asking only whether the chatbot could answer a question, I looked at whether the same concept remained understandable when the audience changed. The online shopping example gave me a way to test explanations against a familiar situation.

Relevance to my audience

For engineering teams and hiring managers, this project demonstrates how I test AI-generated explanations, consider the needs of different users, and communicate technical ideas. It also shows a different skill from my first artifact, which focused on research and visual communication.
